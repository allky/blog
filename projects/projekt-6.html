👤 Facial recognition using siamese neural networks

🧬 Deep learning 📅 May 2025 🏫 School Project

✨ ABOUT PROJECT

This project was developed as part of the Neural Networks course during my studies at FIIT STU. I worked on the project as a team with my classmates Lenka & Martin ♡.

The main goal of the project was to develop and evaluate an advanced face verification system. The core approach involved training Siamese Neural Networks to learn high-quality facial embeddings capable of accurately distinguishing between different human faces. The system's task is to verify whether two separate facial images belong to the same person or not.

A key contribution of our work was a comprehensive comparison between networks trained using classical Triplet Margin Loss and a state-of-the-art alternative called Shadow Loss (based on recent research). Additionally, we systematically explored the effects of different model architectures, embedding sizes, and various hyperparameters on the overall verification performance.

✨ WHAT WE DID

I participated across the entire deep learning workflow—from deep dataset exploration to architecture experimentation, hyperparameter optimization, and extensive validation.

  • 1. Technical Literature & Research
    We conducted a review of state-of-the-art deep learning losses. We focused our research on a recent paper introducing Shadow Loss ↗, which claims to provide major advantages in memory efficiency and faster convergence by projecting embeddings into lower-dimensional spaces. Based on this, we designed an experimental framework to validate these claims against the traditional Triplet Margin Loss baseline.
  • 2. Dataset & EDA
    We used the popular Labeled Faces in the Wild (LFW) dataset, containing over 13,000 face images collected from the web. During exploratory analysis, we examined the distribution of images across classes, inspected image resolutions and quality, and identified class imbalance issues; as most classes (persons) contain only 1 image (~71% of the dataset).
  • 3. Data preprocessing & Triplet Sampling
    We split the dataset by identity — 80% training, 10% validation, 10% testing — ensuring that no identity appeared in more than one subset. To support triplet-based training, we implemented an OnlineTripletDataset class that dynamically samples anchor, positive, and negative image triplets. We also prepared labeled image pairs from the test set for the final verification stage.
  • 4. Model Architecture
    We implemented and benchmarked two convolutional neural network backbones:
    • Standard ResNet18: A fully-fledged architecture utilizing residual blocks (convolutional layers, batch normalization, and ReLU) to prevent vanishing gradients.




    • Simplified ResNet18: A lightweight, custom version with fewer residual blocks and reduced channel expansion, built to evaluate performance-to-cost trade-offs.




  • 5. Experiments & Hyperparameter Optimization
    We ran a series of targeted experiments using Bayesian hyperparameter sweeps (via Weights & Biases) optimizing batch size, embedding size, learning rate, margin, and weight decay — with validation EER as the target metric. All experiments used the AdamW optimizer with a CosineAnnealingLR scheduler over 10 epochs. The experiments included: Shadow Loss vs. Triplet Loss comparison, training on only valid classes (those with 2+ images), Simplified ResNet18 evaluation, and final best-model training.
  • 6. Evaluation
    Models were evaluated using ROC curves and biometric verification metrics: TAR@FAR=10%/5%/1%, AUC, and EER. Shadow Loss consistently outperformed Triplet Loss across all metrics — achieving a better AUC (0.884 vs. 0.811) and lower EER (0.194 vs. 0.262). Our best model, trained for 15 epochs with a margin of 0.3, reached an EER of 0.148 and AUC of 0.926.

✨ WHAT DID I LEARN?

This project gave me hands-on experience with deep metric learning and the challenges of building a face verification system from scratch. I learned how Siamese networks learn to compare rather than classify, and how sensitive embedding quality is to the choice of loss function and hyperparameters.

The most valuable insight was seeing how Shadow Loss — by projecting embeddings into a lower-dimensional space — led to faster convergence and stronger verification performance than the standard Triplet Loss. It also taught me that this faster convergence comes with a trade-off: Shadow Loss was more prone to overfitting when hyperparameters weren't carefully tuned. Understanding those subtle dynamics between optimization speed, generalization, and model complexity was the biggest takeaway from this project.
