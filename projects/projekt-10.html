🔍 Manga search engine

🔍 Information retrieval ⛏️ Web scraping 🗣️ Natural language processing 📅 May 2024 🏫 School Project

✨ ABOUT PROJECT

  • 1. Crawling & Scraping
    I built a crawler that respects robots.txt and navigates through 18 manga genre categories on MyAnimeList, collecting up to 5 pages of links per category. The scraper then downloaded the HTML of each manga page into individual .txt files, resulting in a folder of 5,639 scraped pages.
  • 2. Parsing
    A custom regex-based parser extracted structured fields from the raw HTML — including title, genres, authors, serialization, volumes, chapters, status, publication dates, and synopsis — and saved everything into a single manga_results.csv. Fields like authors and publication date ranges required additional handling beyond simple regex matching.
  • 3. Wikipedia Enrichment with PySpark
    To enrich the dataset, I used PySpark to process 20 Wikipedia XML dump files. Pages were first filtered using relevance signals (infobox templates, keyword presence), then further processed to extract publisher names and main characters using multiple regex patterns. The extracted data was joined back to the original dataset by title, enriching approximately 360 out of 5,600 records.
  • 4. Indexing — Two Approaches
    I implemented indexing in two ways. The manual indexer (my_indexer.py) preprocesses all text through tokenization, stopword removal, and lemmatization, then computes TF, IDF, and TF-IDF for every term across all documents, storing the result in an inverted index CSV. The Elasticsearch indexer (indexer.py) preprocesses and loads the same data into a local ES instance, with a concatenated field across all attributes to support phrase search.
  • 5. Search
    Both searchers support boolean queries with AND / OR / NOT operators. The manual searcher ranks results by accumulated TF-IDF score. The Elasticsearch searcher additionally supports field-specific filtering, phrase search, and numeric range queries (e.g. title: girl AND volumes: =10).

✨ EVALUATION

The Elasticsearch searcher was evaluated on 5 queries using Precision, Recall, and F1 against a gold standard built from MyAnimeList search results. Specific field queries (like genre: comedy AND title: city or title: girl AND volumes: =10) achieved perfect scores — Precision, Recall, and F1 all at 1.0. Broader phrase queries performed worse, since MyAnimeList boosts title matches while the system searched across all concatenated fields, leading to mismatched ranking.

✨ WHAT DID I LEARN?

This project gave me hands-on experience with the full information retrieval pipeline — from ethical web crawling and regex-based extraction to building both a manual and a production-grade search index. Working with PySpark on large XML dumps taught me how to think about filtering and efficiency at scale, where processing every document fully would be impractical.

The evaluation phase was especially valuable — comparing my results to a gold standard made it clear how much ranking strategy matters, and why searching a concatenated field behaves very differently from a title-boosted system like MyAnimeList. It was a good reminder that correct results and well-ranked results are two different problems.
